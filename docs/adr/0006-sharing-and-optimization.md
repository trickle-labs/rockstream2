# ADR 0006: shared state and lifetime workload optimization

**Status:** Proposed; simple sharing/rules first, adaptive strategies individually gated. **Date:** 2026-11-25. **Owners:** optimizer, storage and runtime roles.

## Decision

Maintain a canonical workload graph with reusable subexpressions and arrangements behind the SQL-first product. After a digest lookup compare full canonical descriptors, including semantic/key/equality version and tenant/security context. Keep physical layout variants separate from logical equivalence. Record each consumer's next required cut, retained-history protection, publication group and maintenance policy; reject incompatible reuse or make a deliberate copy.

Use logical MVCC to let fast and slow consumers read different retained cuts while shared state advances. A shared arrangement does not force unrelated groups to publish together. Actual data dependencies, complete source transactions and explicitly atomic publication groups still constrain compatible cuts. Each consumer declares freshness/scheduling, required cut coverage and replay floor, history age/bytes, maintenance and recovery budgets, and an explicit exhaustion policy. Budget exhaustion requires additional capacity, isolation, admission control or an authorized expiry/resnapshot transition; it never silently discards promised history. Physical checkpoints need not coincide, but exact covered logical cuts and source lineage must.

Optimize total lifetime cost under SLOs: maintenance, state/history, object requests/bytes, metadata, exchange, compaction, serving, recovery, dormant retention, wake-up/catch-up and changeover duplication. Begin with rule-based choices and measured statistics. First-order IVM is the semantic/performance baseline. Prototype a bounded eligible partial-aggregation/factorization family in G0 and qualify it in G3; keep general higher-order derivatives and adaptive selection independently gated in G5. Every optimization has explicit semantic side conditions and tests. Local recomputation may be the best correct strategy when the affected region is small or incremental state is more expensive.

Sharing is an economic optimization, not an obligation of semantic equivalence. Compare a shared base plus the history retained by all consumers with isolated bases and their separate retention, including CPU, latency, requests, network, compaction, build, recovery and temporary duplication. Charge the marginal history caused by a slow consumer and enforce physical pool limits independently. G0 P0.8 includes this comparison for 100 and 1,000 overlapping views; G3 qualifies the production path. Use isolation for workload classes where sharing fails the declared total-cost or resource criteria.

Support explicit or rule-based isolation with the first shared arrangements. Use a pinned snapshot, protected replay, equality at a complete cut and atomic consumer-binding/plan activation with retention-obligation handoff. Activation must compare the live consumer publication head and cursor at the verified cut and fence old-plan attempts; progress after verification requires catch-up and renewed verification, never a cursor regression or duplicate effects. Matching the endpoint is insufficient: certify every still-promised historical cut and complete operator recovery/replay requirements, or retain authoritative references to the necessary old shared history. Keep original obligations active until that replacement protection is authoritative; preserve previous state for remaining obligations, valid readers and the rollback window. Include retained old-history cost in any claimed savings until it is legitimately released. Qualify failures and ambiguous cutovers, not just the successful copy. The [runtime isolation protocol](../design/runtime-and-operations.md#isolating-a-shared-consumer) defines this path. Automatic adaptation follows with hysteresis, cooldown, minimum benefit, hard build/duplicate-state budgets and estimate-confidence reporting. A fast plan that cannot be rebuilt within the recovery contract is not admissible.

For scheduled consumers, cost continuous maintenance against retained deltas, bounded replay and snapshot rebuild. Only a qualified strategy that preserves source continuity, promised historical cuts, freshness and recovery may replace maintained state. Initial exact-state persistence remains the baseline until a replacement recipe is proved; sleep is not permission to lose required history. See [ADR 0010](0010-operation-and-hibernation.md).

## Alternatives and tradeoffs

Independent per-view circuits simplify ownership but waste state on overlapping workloads. Aggressive global sharing can couple failures, hold expensive history and undermine tenant isolation. The design shares within compatible boundaries and permits duplication when it reduces total cost or isolates an SLO. Cross-tenant sharing is disabled initially.

A one-shot SQL cost optimizer ignores update/delete rates and future state. A universal higher-order strategy can explode auxiliary state. A learned/adaptive optimizer from day one adds hard-to-reproduce decisions before telemetry exists. The staged approach makes each strategy explainable and reversible.

## Evidence

G0 compares the grouped-join example with first-order and eligible factorized plans and adds a bounded sharing-economics/isolation experiment at 100 and 1,000 views. G3 repeats shared and unshared execution through the production path with independent schedules, slow consumers, online drops, sleep/resume and retained snapshots. Qualify the initial factorization family through public SQL, deletes, numeric/NULL semantics, historical cuts and crash recovery. Count metadata/history/build and wake-up costs as well as steady-state RAM. G5 promotes each broader strategy only when exact results, recovery and lifetime cost improve on a predeclared eligible workload. Do not generalize research's favorable results into a universal gain: [F-IVM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08583) and [shared arrangements](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.02639) are algorithmic precedents.

The algorithm eligibility and optimizer contract are in [semantics and compiler](../design/semantics-and-compiler.md).
